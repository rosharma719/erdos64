# Erdős–Gyárfás: strategic assessment and program (17 November 2026)

**Status.** This is a strategy document, not a proof. The conjecture is still open. Every
statement is tagged **PROVED** (argued here, short), **EXTERNAL** (literature, with
source), **COMPUTED** (finite, reproducible from `../passes/14_rooted_stress_test/`) or
**TARGET** (a precise statement we propose to prove; not established).

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Disproof looks unlikely.** Every known way of avoiding powers of two inflates the
   order much faster than it can afford (§2). The independent 2026 search campaigns
   found nothing in any habitat swept so far.
2. **The dossier's primary track, rooted gluing across 3-cuts, cannot be the whole
   proof.** Root-robustness fails for an explicit 3-connected cubic graph on 16 vertices
   (§3). Gluing works across every 3-cut except those with a small triangle-based side,
   and the hardest known instances consist almost entirely of such sides.
3. **The only technique known to produce exact cycle lengths at scale is expansion plus
   absorption** (Liu–Montgomery; Friedman–Krivelevich). The key observation (§4) is that
   minimum degree 3 is exactly what turns short cycles into length adjusters. It is also
   exactly what every known dyadic-free "flexible" construction lacks.
4. **Recommended program:** Pillar A (bounded-degree expanders with δ ≥ 3: exact even
   lengths across a linear range), then Pillar B (gluing expander pieces with
   flexible-length transit), plus the finite band n ∈ [50, 62] for cubic {4,8,16}-freeness.

## 1. Literature state (verified 17 Nov 2026)

- **EXTERNAL.** Ducoffe–Dumitru, arXiv:2609.28594: a minimal counterexample is
  2-edge-connected and almost biconnected; verified for general graphs ≤ 40, cubic ≤ 48
  and bipartite ≤ 66.
- **EXTERNAL.** Carr, arXiv:2605.22844: minimal counterexamples are predominantly cubic.
  An independent audit in the `mcolsen/erdos-gyarfas` archive confirms this, sharpens it
  to ≥ 2/3 cubic, and reports (unrefereed) cubic ≥ 50 and bipartite ≥ 57.
- **EXTERNAL (unrefereed).** The same archive proves an *α-block equivalence*: EG fails
  iff some 2-connected graph with at most one degree-2 vertex avoids every power of two
  up to its own order.
- **EXTERNAL.** Friedman–Krivelevich, *Combinatorica* 41 (2021), arXiv:1912.11011.
  - In an α-expander on n ≥ n₀ vertices, every ℓ ∈ [C log n, n/C] has a cycle of length
    in [ℓ, ℓ+A], with A = O(1/α); the additive error is optimal.
  - β-graphs have cycles of every length in [b₁ log n, (1−b₂)n].
  - Cubic graphs are not β-graphs for small β, so this does not settle cubic expanders.
- **EXTERNAL.** Liu–Montgomery (dossier R4): EG holds under large constant average
  degree. Their exact-length "adjuster" machinery needs large degree.
- **EXTERNAL.** Heckman–Krakovski: 3-connected cubic planar graphs. Also known: claw-free
  and P₈/P₁₃-free classes, diameter 2, and odd-order Cayley graphs (Burkhart,
  arXiv:2611.05060).
- **EXTERNAL (unrefereed).** Cubic graphs with no cycle of length 4, 8, 16 exist at
  82–126 vertices. A 370-vertex graph avoids 4…32, and a 26,550-vertex graph avoids 4…64
  (Zenodo 23886386; `mcolsen/erdos-gyarfas`).
- Search found no EG result for bounded treewidth, bounded genus beyond the plane, or
  bounded-degree expanders.

## 2. The growth reformulation (why disproof looks unlikely)

**PROVED.** Let f(k) be the minimum order of a graph with δ ≥ 3 and no cycle of length
2², …, 2^k. Then

> **EG ⟺ f(k) ≥ 2^(k+1) for every k ≥ 2.**

*Proof.* A counterexample G on n vertices avoids 4, …, 2^K for K = ⌊log₂ n⌋, so
f(K) ≤ n < 2^(K+1). Conversely, a minimiser for f(K) < 2^(K+1) has no room for any
cycle of length 2^(K+1) or more, so it avoids every power of two. ∎

| k | threshold 2^(k+1) | known lower bound for f(k) | best known construction |
|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 2 | 8 | 10 (Petersen is optimal) | 10 |
| 3 | 16 | ≥ 16 (EG verified to 40) | 24 (cubic) |
| 4 | 32 | ≥ 33 general, ≥ 50 cubic | 82 |
| 5 | 64 | ≥ 41 general, ≥ 50 cubic | 370 |
| 6 | 128 | same | 26,550 |

- **Small k:** a counterexample needs f(k) to dip *below* 2^(k+1). For k ≤ 4 that is
  already excluded.
- **Margin:** the construction-to-threshold ratio climbs 1.25, 1.5, 2.6, 5.8, 207. Each
  extra forbidden power costs far more than the factor 2 it may spend.
- **Smallest open band:** k = 5, i.e. graphs on 41–63 vertices avoiding {4,8,16,32}.
  - For cubic graphs this needs a {4,8,16}-free cubic graph on 50–62 vertices.
  - The only known ones have ≥ 82 vertices.
  - Settling cubic {4,8,16}-freeness on 50–62 vertices would close the cubic k = 5 band
    outright. This is the one finite computation worth funding (SAT/SMS scale, days per
    order; not attempted here).

## 3. Rooted gluing: what holds and where it breaks (Pass XIV)

Call G *root-robust* if every vertex is avoided by some dyadic cycle.

**PROVED (gluing lemma).** Let G be a 3-connected cubic graph and δ(A) a nontrivial
3-edge cut, with sides A and B.
- Contracting B gives a 3-connected cubic graph G_A, and contracting A gives G_B.
- For r ∈ A, a dyadic cycle of G_B that avoids the contracted vertex lies inside B, so
  it avoids r.
- Hence if G_A and G_B are root-robust, so is G. Root-robustness then implies EG.

**PROVED (minimal counterexample shape).** Let (G, r) be a minimum-order counterexample to
"3-connected cubic on ≥ 10 vertices implies root-robust". Then for every nontrivial
3-cut, the side B not containing r is one of the following:
- a triangle; or
- a 7-vertex piece whose contraction is K₄ with two vertices truncated, or K₃,₃ with one
  vertex truncated.

In particular, no 5-vertex sides occur.

**COMPUTED.**
- **Small cases:** all 3-connected cubic graphs through 16 vertices were generated from K₄
  by bridging. The counts 1, 2, 4, 14, 57, 341, 2828 match the known sequence. They are
  root-robust except for:
  - K₄;
  - the two 8-vertex graphs above;
  - one 16-vertex graph, `OJO?_U?_kA?@H?B?___@H`. This is K₄ with two vertices replaced
    by the 7-vertex piece, and vertex 7 lies on every 4-, 8- and 16-cycle (checked by
    exhaustive NetworkX enumeration).
- **Gadget recursion:** feeding the 15-vertex piece back into the same K₄ recursion gives
  only robust 32-vertex graphs.
- **Hard instances:** every root is avoided, with independently checked witness cycles,
  in all of the following:
  - all 4/8-free cubic graphs on 24 and 26 vertices (4 and 23);
  - 21 + 1 graphs on 28–30 vertices from the archive's `tr8zero` files, plus all 48
    C8-free girth-5 graphs on 30 vertices;
  - 100 large vertex-transitive survivors;
  - 18 {4,8,16}-free graphs on 82–126 vertices;
  - the 370-vertex graph (by 64-cycles);
  - the 26,550-vertex graph (by 128-cycles).
- **Triangle density:** 17 of the 18 {4,8,16}-free graphs, and the 370-vertex graph, have
  59–90% of their vertices in triangles.

**Consequence.** Rooted gluing reduces EG for 3-connected cubic graphs to prime graphs
*plus* triangle- and gadget-sided cuts. Those sides are where both the root-critical
exceptions and the hardest known graphs live. Contracting a triangle turns "avoid r"
into "avoid r and t", so the hypothesis does not close. The dossier's rooted *prime*
conjecture survives everything here, but a proof of it would not finish the job.

## 4. The structural reason exactness is possible at δ ≥ 3

**PROVED (adjuster lemma).** Let δ(G) ≥ 3 and let Q be an induced cycle of length q.
- Pick u, v on Q at arc distance ⌊(q−1)/2⌋. The two u–v arcs differ in length by
  s = q − 2⌊(q−1)/2⌋, which is 1 or 2.
- Since Q is induced and the degree is ≥ 3, both u and v have an edge leaving Q.
- So a cycle that enters Q at u, leaves at v and is otherwise disjoint from Q has two
  versions whose lengths differ by s.
- A cycle threaded through M disjoint adjusters realises every length in
  [L, L + Σsᵢ], in steps of 1 if some sᵢ = 1 and in steps of 2 otherwise.

**Why this matters.**
- The dossier's dyadic-free theta necklaces are subcubic: their degree-2 vertices have no
  free edge, so no adjuster can be entered there.
- I expect Friedman–Krivelevich's optimal additive error to be witnessed by
  subdivision-type examples too. Not checked.
- Minimum degree 3 is the hypothesis that converts "a cycle of length within A of ℓ"
  into "a cycle of length exactly ℓ". This explains why the conjecture needs δ ≥ 3 and
  where a proof must use it.

## 5. Program

**Pillar A — TARGET A.** For every α > 0 and Δ there are n₀ and C such that every
α-expander G with 3 ≤ δ(G) ≤ Δ(G) ≤ Δ on n ≥ n₀ vertices contains a cycle of every even
length in [n/C², n/C]. In particular it contains a cycle whose length is a power of two.

Proof plan (standard tools except step 4):
1. Greedily take M ≈ n/(K log n) vertex-disjoint induced cycles of length O(log n) as
   adjusters. This uses n·O(1/K) vertices, so a robust α/2-expanding core survives.
2. Thread them into one path R using links of length O(log n/α) in the core. The base
   length of R is O(n/K), and it is adjustable by [0, 2M].
3. Find a long path P in the rest of the core (DFS in expanders).
4. Join the two ends of R to P at chosen positions with landing precision
   D = n/(K' log n), using connections of length O(log log n/α). This is the technical
   heart: Friedman–Krivelevich's closing argument with prescribed endpoints.
5. Choose K' ≫ K so that D + O(log log n) ≤ 2M. The adjusters then absorb the landing
   error exactly, for any even target length in the range.

Value: new as far as the search found. It would settle EG for bounded-degree expanders
with δ ≥ 3, in a unified proof.

**Pillar B — the crux, speculative.** A minimal counterexample has average degree
bounded by Liu–Montgomery, so it is not a large-degree object.
- Expander decomposition splits it into φ-expanders, φ = 1/polylog n, with few edges
  between pieces.
- *Case 1, a large piece:* run Pillar A with polylogarithmic losses. Adjusters avoid the
  piece boundary, where degree drops.
- *Case 2, all pieces small:* the quotient graph has many pieces of bounded "transit
  spectrum". Each piece crossed adds a choosable length from an interval, like a
  subdivision with flexible path lengths. This is Liu–Montgomery's setting with
  adjustable subdivisions; the multi-scale gadget calculus of the hard examples lives
  here.
- **Falsifiable intermediate claim:** a piece with internal δ ≥ 3 and k terminals has, for
  some terminal pair, a transit-length set containing an interval of ratio ≥ 2 once it is
  large enough relative to k.
- Known counter-pressure: diamond chains give a ratio of only 4/3, so the claim needs the
  global count of pieces, not single pieces.

**Finite track (one item).** Cubic {4,8,16}-freeness on 50–62 vertices (§2).

**Stop doing.**
- New rooted censuses: the rooted 3-connected statement is false at 16 vertices.
- Generic small-order enumeration: the frontier sits at 40–50 vertices in the literature.
- Ad hoc gadget passes without a growth or absorption argument.

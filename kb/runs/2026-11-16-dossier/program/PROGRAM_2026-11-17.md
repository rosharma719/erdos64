## 2. The decisive new observation: path-anchored adjusters

The one missing lemma from Pass XV, the Attachable Adjuster Lemma, splits into two
problems:

- **attachment:** reaching two near-opposite points of a short cycle;
- **gcd:** the available length steps must not all share an odd prime factor or a
  factor 4.

**Attachment goes away if adjusters sit on a path.**
- Let P be the long path that the Friedman–Krivelevich construction builds anyway. Every
  interior vertex of P uses two of its ≥ 3 edges, so it has a free edge. This is true at
  δ = 3 and false at δ = 2.
- An *ear* is a path R outside P joining P-vertices at P-distance j. Using R instead of
  that piece of P changes the length by r − j, where r = |R|.
- Ear endpoints can be anywhere on P, so no position control is needed. What remains is
  the **gcd problem** alone.

**Gcd Lemma (TARGET G, "local Lyngsie–Merker").**
- Let P be a long path in a bounded-degree α-expander with δ ≥ 3, placed as in the FK
  construction.
- Among O(1) disjoint, non-crossing ears on any prescribed long segment of P, the steps
  r − j are not all divisible by 4, and not all divisible by any fixed odd prime p.

Why this is plausible and checkable:
- Suppose all ear steps on a long segment are ≡ 0 (mod p). Then the segment plus its ears
  forms a "mod-p graded" structure: every cycle built from the segment and its ears has a
  length determined mod p.
- Lyngsie–Merker show that this kind of rigidity cannot hold globally in large
  3-connected cubic graphs; their theta-necklace switching produces every residue.
- Target G asks for the same conclusion *locally*, along a path inside an expander. That
  is a clean, falsifiable, purely combinatorial statement, and Lyngsie–Merker's method is
  the obvious tool.

Parallel, independent tracks:

- **F1 (finite frontier).** Show that no cubic graph on 50–62 vertices avoids
  {C4, C8, C16}.
  - This closes the cubic k = 5 band: a counterexample there would have to be such a
    graph, and the smallest known one has 82 vertices.
  - It is SAT/SMS work at about 1–6 days per order, using the method validated in
    `mcolsen/erdos-gyarfas`.
  - p ≈ 0.7 with the tooling. It needs compute and a nauty/SAT build, which this
    environment blocked.
- **F2 (falsification of Target G).** Look for a bounded-degree δ ≥ 3 expander with a
  mod-p graded long path, constructed by theory first.
  - p ≈ 0.1. If it succeeds it kills Pillar A as designed and redirects everything,
    which is cheap to know early.

## 4. Why this order is optimal

1. **Front-load finished theorems.** M0 is likely to work, is publishable as a short
   note, and builds the threading machinery that M1 and A need. A partial run still
   produces a result.
2. **Place the single bet early.** M2 decides the value of everything downstream (A, S,
   B). Attempt it right after M1, with F2 running alongside as its falsifier. Stop
   investing in S and B until M2 is resolved one way or the other.
3. **Do not spend on dominated tracks.** These cannot reach EG without M2-type exactness:
   rooted censuses, new gadget passes, the rooted-prime conjecture, and small-order
   enumeration below the published frontier.
4. **F1 is the only computation worth funding.** It is decisive for the smallest open
   band, where a counterexample is a priori most likely (§2 of the strategy note).

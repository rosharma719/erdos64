# Clean three-port pieces: a complete small classification (2026-11-16)

**Status.** Two computer-assisted results, each checked against an
independent earlier computation in this track. Hand proofs are not written yet.
Not machine-checked, not reviewed. **No claim about the conjecture itself.**

## Definition

A **clean 3-pole** is a connected simple graph with exactly three vertices of
degree 2 (the *ports*), every other vertex of degree 3, and no cycle whose
length is a power of two.

Deleting any non-cut vertex from a connected cubic Erdős–Gyárfás
counterexample leaves a clean 3-pole. Conversely, a cubic graph `H` with a vertex `v` such that `H − v` is a
clean 3-pole is exactly a *dyadically root-critical* pair `(H, v)` in the sense
of `prime_pole_obstructions.md` and `root_orbit_switching.md`.

## Result 2 — the triangle closure is finite [COMPUTED, signature-level]

Call a 3-pole *triangle-built* if it is a single vertex, or a triangle whose
three corners are triangle-built 3-poles (each corner keeps one port as an
outer port and uses two for the triangle edges). These are exactly the 3-poles
reachable from one vertex by repeatedly replacing a vertex by a triangle.

A cycle or port-to-port path enters each corner at most once, so the
**signature** (internal cycle lengths, three port-pair path-length sets) of a
composite is determined by the signatures of its corners.
`scripts/triangle_closure.py` composes signatures over all corner orders and
port roles.

**Result.** The clean triangle-built 3-poles have exactly 1, 3, 7 and 15
vertices, one signature each (vertex, triangle, skew H7, H15), at every size.

*Why every size.* A clean composite needs clean corners, since corner cycles are
composite cycles. The enumeration ran to 63 vertices and found none above 15.
Three corners from `{1, 3, 7, 15}` total at most 45, so no clean
triangle-built 3-pole above 15 vertices can exist.

*Why it stops at 15.* The only way to reach 31 is 15 + 15 + 1. Its triangle
cycle then has lengths filling [9,31], [11,31] or [13,31], depending on the
port roles. All three windows contain 16. In general the shortest port path
grows by 1 or 2 per level while the longest grows to `N − 1`, so the
triangle-cycle window eventually spans a factor of 2.

The uniform H7 is clean but **not** triangle-built: it comes from a different
8-vertex base.

## What this suggests

**Conjecture T (three-port tower).** The only clean 3-poles are the triangle,
the two H7 gadgets and H15. In particular every clean 3-pole has at most 15
vertices.

- T implies the cubic Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture. A connected cubic
  counterexample has at least 50 vertices (no cubic counterexample of order
  ≤ 48, Ducoffe–Dumitru, arXiv:2609.28594; cubic orders are even). Deleting a
  vertex that is not a cut vertex leaves a connected graph whose three
  degree-2 vertices are the deleted vertex's neighbours: a clean 3-pole on at
  least 49 vertices.
- T is falsifiable by the same enumeration at 21, 23, 25 vertices.
- If T holds, every piece hanging off a cyclic 3-edge cut of a counterexample
  has **interval** port path lengths. That is what lets the additive calculus
  of `cycle_space_obstruction.md` and `three_cut_prime_transfer.md` apply. See
  `research_brief_additive_reduction.md`.

## Correction to an earlier remark

In conversation it was suggested that H7 answers Ducoffe–Dumitru's question
about allowing several low-degree vertices. It does not: a triangle with one
pendant edge already has three vertices of degree at most 2, a vertex of
degree 3, and no power-of-two cycle. The non-trivial case of their question is
two low-degree vertices. No clean graph with exactly two degree-2 vertices
and the rest of degree ≥ 3 exists on 8 to 13 vertices (the C4-free two-pole
enumeration in `richness_findings.md`; none of those pieces is C8-free).

## Reproduce

```sh
cc -O3 -o cycle_check scripts/cycle_check.c
for N in 7 9 11 13 15 17 19; do E=$(( (3*N-3)/2 )); geng -q -c -f -d2 -D3 $N $E:$E | ./cycle_check 2> clean_$N.g6; done
python3 scripts/identify_three_poles.py clean_*.g6
python3 scripts/triangle_closure.py 63
```

`N = 19` took about 50 seconds on one core. `cycle_check` prints the clean
graphs on standard error.
